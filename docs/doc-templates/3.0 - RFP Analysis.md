## 3.0 RFP Analysis

RFP Analysis is the structured, analytical decomposition of the buyer’s source documents. By translating narratives and requirements into discrete parameters, the RFP Analysis Engine establishes a multidimensional baseline covering the following functional areas:

1. **Project Summary**
   Provide a concise summary of the project’s purpose and goals.

2. **Bid Opportunities**
   Briefly identify the areas, components, phases, or optional scopes on which vendors may bid. If the RFP contains only one bidding area, state the project’s primary theme and goal.

3. **Scope of Work**
   Present the key areas of the RFP’s Scope of Work in a table.

4. **Vendor Experience and References**
   Identify prior-experience and reference requirements relevant to the agency and Scope of Work. Highlight any required signatures or other specific reference details that the vendor, based on the Vendor Synopsis, may not possess or fully demonstrate.

5. **Proposal Format**
   Outline every required part of the proposal response. Structure this section so it can serve as a checklist for preparing a complete and compliant proposal.

6. **Project Team**
   Identify the required team composition, roles, qualifications, certifications, resumes, references, and staffing expectations. State whether signed references are required.

   Use only the roles specified in the RFP. If roles are not specified, propose the smallest practical team—preferably no more than five members unless the RFP clearly requires otherwise.

   Conclude with a table of required resource roles. Include a final column explaining how each role will support the deliverables and Scope of Work and contribute to project success. Do not invent or infer project needs that the RFP does not identify.

7. **Budget and Pricing**
   Identify whether the contract uses fixed-price, time-and-materials, or another pricing model. Include the required pricing structure, cost template, payment terms, budget information, and contract term when stated.

8. **Requirements**
   Tabulate all explicitly stated technical, operational, platform, integration, security, compliance, and performance requirements. Include expected deliverables only when they are stated in the RFP. Do not introduce unstated requirements.

9. **Work Plan and Schedule**
   Identify the anticipated project duration and tabulate key schedule information, including the procurement timeline, implementation timeline, milestones, and deadlines. When possible, list the hours specified for identified tasks. If the RFP does not provide hours, estimate the minimum or optimal hours required to achieve the stated goals and clearly label them as estimates.

10. **Technology Stack**
    Address this area only when the RFP provides relevant information. Identify the stated current-state platforms, tools, products, technology vendors, hosting arrangements, locations, and delivery preferences. When defined, also describe the stated future-state platforms, tools, products, technology vendors, and hosting arrangements.

11. **Contract Scope**
    Summarize the terms of engagement, including contract duration, insurance, legal and compliance requirements, ownership, confidentiality, data protection and privacy, warranties, renewals, termination provisions, and mandatory forms.

12. **Approach and Methodology**
    Identify any approach or methodology required or evaluated by the RFP. If none is stated, provide brief guidance for developing an appropriate proposal approach without presenting it as an RFP requirement.

13. **Gaps, Ambiguities, Risks, and Clarifications**
    Identify gaps, ambiguities, risks, inconsistencies, and clarification questions that should be addressed before proposal submission.

14. **Solution Expectations**
    Address this area only when the RFP provides sufficient detail. Determine whether the buyer is seeking a turnkey implementation, an existing vendor solution, an expandable configurable platform, or a custom-built solution.

15. **Vendor Fitness**
    Using the Vendor Synopsis, assess whether the opportunity is a good fit for the vendor. For a multicomponent RFP, identify the components for which the vendor is best qualified. Highlight capability gaps and explain what the vendor would need—such as personnel, certifications, experience, products, or partners—to qualify and compete effectively.
